This project is part of a larger effort. Parallel teams are building a pocket finder and an orthology predictor. A follow-up project will use your engine to compute pocket fingerprints for similarity search and to dock ligands against pockets across the whole AlphaFold database (AFDB).
For this task, assume that the correct pocket was found (this is solved in the pocket finder project).
Build a fast, physics-inspired docking engine that predicts protein–small-molecule binding poses. It must clearly beat state-of-the-art in accuracy and speed. Deep learning should not be the main strategy, and training a new deep-learning structure model is out of scope. Instead it could use things like empirical or force-field-style energy terms, explicit pose sampling, local optimisation, and a modest number of interpretable parameters fitted to data.
Speed is an explicit research goal, not an afterthought. You are encouraged to rework the docking algorithm itself to make it faster, for example its search strategy, scoring-grid evaluation and early termination. You are also encouraged to explore GPU acceleration alongside multi-core CPU optimisation, so that docking becomes fast enough for proteome scale.
The key application is docking into pockets identified in AlphaFold models, so the method should have a specific mode for these structures. Docking into apo predicted structures is known to work much worse than into holo crystal structures. Closing this gap is the main focus. Possible strategies include:
co-folding the protein with a generic molecule or probe to obtain a holo-like pocket before docking;
sampling pocket dynamics with BioEmu2 or fast MD and docking against the resulting ensemble
Exploring co-folding pocket strategies that does not require to fold the entire protein to have a fast but accurate method
These are only suggestions. Be creative and pursue whatever is most promising.
